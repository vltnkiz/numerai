Glossary: Performance Stationarity & Era Boosting

Terms used consistently across every lesson in this course. Print or bookmark this — it will keep growing.

Era
Numerai's unit of time in its dataset — roughly, a period during which market conditions are treated as consistent. Performance and feature-target relationships can differ substantially from one era to the next.
Performance stationarity
The property that a model's sequence of good/bad eras is "memoryless" — behaves like a biased coin flip, with no long clumps of consecutive losses (or wins). A narrower, applied version of the general statistics term "stationary process."
Source: Performance Stationarity
Autocorrelation
How much a sequence's value at one point predicts its value shortly after. Near zero for a memoryless sequence; positive when good/bad eras tend to follow their own kind (a losing era makes the next era more likely to lose too). The mathematical signature of non-stationary performance.
Sharpe ratio
mean(returns) / std(returns) — average return per unit of volatility. Rewards consistency in magnitude, but blind to order: it scores a scattered set of bad eras identically to the same bad eras all clustered together, as long as the variance matches.
Smart Sharpe
mean(x) / (std(x) × autocorr_penalty(x)) — Sharpe ratio with an added penalty for autocorrelation. Two models with equal plain Sharpe can have different Smart Sharpe if one has clumpier losses.
Source: Performance Stationarity
Non-stationary features
Stock-market features (e.g. value, momentum) whose relationship to the target cycles through working and non-working regimes over time, rather than staying fixed. Named as the upstream cause of non-stationary model performance: a model trained on unstable features inherits that instability.
Era boosting
A training-time technique for gradient-boosted models: after building an initial batch of trees, score per-era correlation, then build the next batch of trees using only the worst-performing eras. Repeating this pushes the model toward more equal performance across eras, rather than just maximizing mean correlation.
Source: Era Boosted Models
proportion (era boosting parameter)
The fraction of eras (ranked worst to best by per-era correlation) kept for the next round of tree-building. richai's original post uses proportion=0.5 — the bottom half of eras by performance.
trees_per_step / num_iters
trees_per_step: how many new trees are added in each era boosting round (10, in the original post). num_iters: how many rounds are run in total (leading to 200 trees over 20 rounds in the original post's headline result).
